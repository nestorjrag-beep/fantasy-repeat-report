A report for PPR fantasy football managers

At which positions do top fantasy finishers repeat the next season? Season-long PPR finishes of QB, RB, WR and TE player-seasons, 2015–2025 NFL regular seasons

Nestor Aguilar

Last season's top finishers repeat only about half the time: 52.4% of top-12 QBs and TEs and top-24 RBs and WRs finished at that level again the next year. Elite running backs are the safest bet (72% of top-5 RBs stayed in the top 24), elite quarterbacks are the least reliable (54%), and receivers are deep enough that you can wait on them. An elite tight end also gives you the biggest edge over an average starter at his position, scoring 60% more points.

Recommendation: spend your first pick on a top-5 running back, take one of the top three tight ends if he is there in the next round or two, wait on quarterback, and build your receiver depth in the middle rounds. Do not pay full price for last year's QB1.

A top finisher is a top-12 QB or TE or a top-24 RB or WR by season PPR points, the usual cut for a weekly starter in a 12-team league. "Repeat" means the same player finishes as a top finisher at the same position the next season.

Finding 1

Only about half of last year's top finishers repeat, and that holds every year

Each season has 72 top finishers (12 QBs, 24 RBs, 24 WRs, 12 TEs). From 2015 through 2024, the share who finished as top finishers again the next season stayed between 47.2% (2017 and 2019) and 59.7% (2021), for 52.4% overall across 720 top finishes. Injuries, role changes and age reset the board about as often as they don't, so last year's final rankings are a weak draft guide on their own.

Finding 2

Elite running backs hold their value best, and elite quarterbacks hold it least

Among the top 5 at each position, 72% of running backs finished top-24 again the next season, compared with 64% of receivers, 64% of tight ends and only 54% of quarterbacks (50 top-5 finishes per position). Across all top finishers the positions look much closer (QB 55.0%, RB 54.2%, WR 52.5%, TE 45.8%), so the RB edge is about the very best backs, not the position as a whole.

Finding 3

The higher a player finishes, the better his odds next year, but even the top 3 miss 30% of the time

Players who finished 1st to 3rd at their position were top finishers again 70.0% of the time. The odds fall steadily with the finish: 57.5% for 4th to 6th, 50.8% for 7th to 12th, 32.7% for 13th to 24th and 4.6% for everyone below 24th. Drafting the very top of last year's list pays off, but nearly one in three of those picks still lets you down.

Finding 4

Receiver is the deepest position, so you can wait on it

Points fall off much more slowly at receiver than at running back. On average, the 22nd to 24th best receivers scored 207.0 PPR points, compared with 178.5 for running backs at the same spots. Further down, receivers ranked 34th to 36th still averaged 175.3 points, while running backs there averaged 137.6. A WR you take in the middle rounds gives up less to the top of the position than an RB taken there does.

Finding 5

An elite tight end is worth the most over a normal starter

The top 3 tight ends each season averaged 242.5 PPR points, compared with 151.6 for tight ends who finished 10th to 12th, an edge of 60.0%. That is the biggest gap at any position: running backs come next at 51.3%, then receivers at 37.6% and quarterbacks at 31.1%. Because most leagues start only one TE and one QB, the elite TE is the scarce advantage and the elite QB is the one you can skip.

About the data

Where the data come from
Weekly player statistics from nflverse, a free public NFL data project: the player_stats release for 2015–2024 and the stats_player release for 2025. nflverse already computes PPR fantasy points for each player in each game (1 point per reception, 0.1 per rushing or receiving yard, 0.04 per passing yard, 6 per rushing or receiving TD, 4 per passing TD, −2 per interception or fumble lost, 2 per two-point conversion).
What one row is
One row of data/player_seasons.csv is one QB, RB, WR or TE in one NFL regular season: total PPR points, games with a stat line, points per game, finish at his position, and whether he was a top finisher the next season. The file has 6,170 rows covering 1,795 players over 11 seasons. The script prep.py builds it from the weekly files.
Which rows are dropped and why
From the 73,901 weekly rows for 2015–2025, I dropped 3,189 playoff rows (fantasy seasons are regular-season only) and 14,059 rows for other positions such as FB, K and defensive players (they are not QB, RB, WR or TE). Seasons before 2015 are left out to keep the period to the modern game, and the 2026 season is left out because it is still in progress. That leaves 56,653 weekly rows, which add up to the 6,170 player-seasons. 2025 player-seasons are kept in the counts and points, but they cannot count toward a repeat rate yet because the 2026 season has not finished.
How each summary number is computed
Player-seasons counted is the number of rows. Top-finisher repeat rate takes every top finisher from a season that has a next season in the data (2015–2024) and divides the number who were top finishers again the next season by that total; a player with no row the next season counts as not repeating. Top-5 repeat rate does the same for players who finished 1st to 5th at their position, and counts them as repeating if they were top finishers (top 12 or top 24) the next season. PPR points per game, top finishers is the total PPR points of all top finishers divided by their total games.
Other definitions
Positional finish ranks every player at a position by season PPR points, across the whole league. Games are weeks with a stat line. A player's team is the team he played the most games for that season. The season and position filters on the dashboard choose which rows are counted, but they never re-rank players.
